Help with the Meah Brachot iPhone app

Questions, problems, or ideas? Email shiron.laleh@gmail.com — or use Settings → Send feedback inside the app, which includes the details we need to help. We read everything.

Frequently asked

How does the app count?
You log what you actually said. The tefillot and meals on the home screen add their brachot in one tap (Shaḥarit, Minḥa, Arvit, a bread meal), and every other bracha has its own row under "Log a bracha." The ring fills toward one hundred. Nothing is assumed.
When does the day reset?
At nightfall (tzet hakochavim) for your location — the halachic day, not midnight. You can choose the stricter tzet in Settings → Day reset. Arvit said after nightfall counts toward the new day.
Why does the count differ for women?
Women don't say the brachot on tallit and tefillin, and the mitzvot said by someone else (an aliyah, a brit, and so on) aren't shown. According to the Sephardi poskim the app follows, a woman who says Ein Kelokeinu fulfills the day's obligation b'dieved, which the app marks accordingly. The Sources screen cites the halachic basis, including the disagreement on the question — as always, ask your own rav.
Which nusach does the app use?
Edut HaMizraḥ, Sefard, or Ashkenaz — chosen during setup and changeable in Settings. Tefillah names and bracha counts follow your choice. Full blessing texts are shown with word-by-word English.
How do reminders work?
Reminders are anchored to that day's actual zmanim for your location, and each one has its own switch and lead time in the Reminders screen: the deadline for Shema, for Birkot Shema, the latest time for Shaḥarit (ḥatzot), Minḥa before nightfall, and Arvit before ḥatzot halayla. Reminders stay silent on Shabbat and Yom Tov and during your quiet hours. They only fire for tefillot you haven't logged yet.
How do streaks work?
Streak mode (Settings → Challenge yourself) counts consecutive halachic days on which you reached one hundred. A flame shows the streak is safe today; an hourglass means today isn't done yet — the streak ends at nightfall unless you finish, and the app warns you 90 minutes before. Shabbat and Yom Tov can't be logged, so they never break a streak: Friday flows into Sunday. It's off by default.
Do I have to share my location?
No. Location is used once to compute zmanim and is never sent anywhere (see the privacy policy). If you'd rather not, choose a city from the list — the zmanim come from the city's coordinates.
I'm traveling — will the times update?
Yes, if "Update automatically when traveling" is on: the app re-checks your location when it notices a new time zone. You can also tap "Use my location" in Settings at any time, or pick the city manually.
Can I use the app in Hebrew?
Yes — the whole interface is available in Hebrew (Settings → Language), with a 12- or 24-hour clock either way.
Is my data backed up or synced?
Your log lives on your phone (and in your normal iPhone backup). There are no accounts and nothing is synced between devices in this version. The app does report anonymous usage statistics — see the privacy policy for exactly what.
I found a mistake in a bracha text or a halachic detail.
Please tell us — email or the in-app feedback form. We take corrections seriously and cite sources in the app.
